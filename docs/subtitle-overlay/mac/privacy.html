Privacy policy

Your subtitles stay yours.

This page covers Subtitle Overlay for Safari on macOS.Last updated 1 October 2026.

The short version.

Subtitle files you open, the timing you set, and every appearance setting stay on your Mac. They are never uploaded anywhere.

The app contacts exactly two services, and nothing else: OpenSubtitles when you search for a subtitle, and PostHog for usage analytics, which stays off until you explicitly turn it on.

There is no account, no sign-in, no advertising, no advertising identifier, no session replay, and no sale or sharing of personal information.

What stays on your Mac.

  • Subtitle files you open or download, and their text.
  • Your timing offset, font, size, colour, background and position settings.
  • The recent-subtitle history used to restore a subtitle when you return to a video.

These live in the extension’s local storage. Removing the app removes them.

Subtitle search.

When you search for a subtitle, the app sends your search terms and filters to api.opensubtitles.com and downloads the file you choose. OpenSubtitles is an independent service with its own privacy policy and its own daily limits.

If you never search, the app never contacts it.

Usage analytics, only if you say yes.

Analytics is off until you explicitly allow it. Declining, skipping, or choosing “Not now” leaves the app fully usable; nothing is withheld. You can change your mind at any time in Settings, General.

If you do allow it, the app creates a random installation identifier at that moment and sends feature-usage events to us.i.posthog.com. Events may include:

  • which features were used, and whether they succeeded or failed;
  • diagnostic values such as an error category and how long something took;
  • the site’s hostname, for example example.com;
  • a one-way SHA-256 hash of the page address, shortened to 16 characters, which lets us tell a repeat visit to the same video from a new one.

The following are never sent to analytics: full page addresses, paths or query strings; subtitle text; what you typed into search; page contents; and any video or audio.

PostHog receives the connection’s IP address and can derive an approximate location from it. Because the installation identifier links events from the same installation, we describe these data as pseudonymous rather than fully anonymous.

Turning sharing off stops further analytics, removes the local identifier, and discards events that have not been sent. It cannot erase records already received by the server; for those, use the deletion request below.

What this version does not do.

Some features exist in the Chrome version of Subtitle Overlay and are not present here. Safari does not give extensions the audio capture they need, so the Mac app contains none of the following, and makes none of the related network requests:

  • no audio capture, speech recognition, or speech model downloads;
  • no licence keys, licence verification, subscriptions, or in-app purchases of any kind;
  • no payment processor is contacted, because the app has no payment path.

Access and deletion.

Ask for a copy of what is held for your installation, or ask for it to be deleted. Email the developer and say which you want.

Analytics records are pseudonymous, so a request may need the installation identifier shown in Settings to locate them. Please do not send anything else to identify yourself.
