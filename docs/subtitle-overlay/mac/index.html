Safari extension for macOS

Your own subtitles, on any video.

Drop an SRT onto the picture and it appears.Set how it looks, fix the timing while you watch.

What it does.

  • Opens SRT, SMI, VTT and ASS, by drag and drop or from the extension.
  • Reads Korean, Japanese and Chinese encodings on its own, so old files open without a mangled first line.
  • Searches OpenSubtitles and loads a result without leaving the page.
  • Size, typeface, colour, background, outline and position are all yours to set, and text scales with the screen.
  • Shift the timing by half a second or a tenth from the keyboard while the video plays.
  • The Sync Timeline lists every line, jumps to any moment, and snaps a line to where the video is now.
  • Come back to a video and restore its last subtitle and timing in one click, kept on your Mac.
  • Interface in 9 languages.

Want to try before deciding? The sample video and subtitle files are timed and ready.

How it is sold.

A single purchase on the Mac App Store. Everything described on this page is included.

There is no subscription, no licence key, no account, and no in-app purchase. The app has no payment path of its own.

Two steps after installing.

  1. Open Safari Settings, choose Extensions, and switch on Subtitle Overlay.
  2. Open a page with a video, click Subtitle Overlay in Safari’s toolbar, and choose Always Allow on This Website.

Safari extensions start with no access to any page, which is why the second step matters. Support walks through it with the usual fixes.

Privacy, briefly.

Subtitle files and settings stay on your Mac. Usage analytics is off until you explicitly turn it on, and declining leaves the app fully usable. The privacy policy spells out every case.
